Source inventory, study period and outcome definition

Bayesian spatiotemporal small-area analysis of COVID-19 vaccination coverage across Texas counties.

Statements about the data were checked against downloaded files. A few statements rest on other material and say so where they occur: a news report (how county is assigned), press reports (the day Phase 1B vaccination began) and search-engine text of two local health departments' reports. Each raw file is listed in data/raw/MANIFEST.csv with its URL, retrieval time, size and SHA-256. The checks are in R/03_verify_vaccination.R; their full output is logs/03_verify_vaccination.log and the tables named verify_*.csv in outputs/tables. Sources were retrieved on 20 November 2026; the DSHS news releases (source I) on 21 November 2026.

1. What the verification found

  1. The proposed outcome (first dose, adults 18+, by county and month) cannot be built for the main rollout. CDC's county file is the only source with an 18+ count, and it has no Texas county data before 22 October 2021: all 254 county rows are zero for every report date from 13 December 2020 to 21 October 2021.
  2. Texas DSHS's own county workbook is the only record of the rollout, and it survives only in archives. DSHS overwrote one workbook each day and has removed it from its site (the old address now returns "not found"). Forty-six editions (19 December 2020 to 10 May 2023) are in the Internet Archive. Forty of them carry a usable county-by-age sheet whose youngest adult band is 16-49, so the adult group the records support is 16+, not 18+.
  3. County counts before 15 April 2021 are incomplete, by a different amount in each county. Until then 5-11% of vaccinated people sat in an "Other" row (county unknown). In the edition of 15 April 2021 that row fell by 662,780 people in one day and county counts jumped by a matching amount. The jump was 1% of the previous count in Harris County, 19% in Tarrant and 25% in Denton. Month-by-month county uptake for January to April 2021 can therefore not be recovered from these files without assumptions about where the unassigned people lived. The main analysis makes none. A separate extension, 03_early_rollout.md, states one, tests it and reports how much depends on it.
  4. CDC and DSHS county counts are not interchangeable. On the same dates CDC's Texas county total is 1.7% to 3.3% above DSHS's, the gap changes over time, and it is very uneven: +36% in Bell County (Fort Hood), +16% in Wichita, +8% in El Paso and Bexar, and lower than DSHS in others. CDC also removed 297,634 people from Texas counties in a single revision on 3 August 2022.
  5. In 16 counties the recorded number of vaccinated adults exceeds the adult population, in several by a wide margin and increasingly so in 2022-2023 (Maverick 234%, Reeves 229%, Webb 189%, Brooks 184% by May 2023). Fourteen are in South Texas or along the Rio Grande; two are in the far-west oil field. Their records cannot be read as coverage of residents.
  6. The archived copies are faithful. A third-party archive of daily CSV copies matches the original workbooks cell for cell on all 21 dates where both exist (5,334 county counts), and a table still served by Harris County Public Health reproduces the final workbook's Harris County count exactly (3,588,122).

Problems found in this source:

  • Late county assignment. See finding 3 and section 4.4.
  • One faulty daily edition (12 March 2021: the "at least one dose" and "fully vaccinated" columns are interchanged, so the one-dose total appears to fall by 2.15 million and return the next day) and one at the end of the daily archive (6 October 2021: 271,000 people moved from counties to "Other", not present in the next original edition). Neither is used.
  • One corrupted age sheet (7 May 2021 edition: race rows pasted into the age sheet; its sums disagree with the county totals in 46 counties). Not used.
  • Excel damaged two age labels. From November 2021 "5-11" and "12-15" are stored as dates (11 May, 15 December). The parser restores them.
  • "Other" keeps growing after the April 2021 fix, from 1.2% of the state total to 3.0% by 2023, so county sums understate statewide first doses by that margin.
  • Reporting lag. By vaccination date, the first edition after a month ended already held 86% to 99% of that month's eventual first doses, typically 94-98% (verify_06b). Counts by report date therefore shift a few percent of each period's doses into the next one.
  • Non-residents. See finding 5 and section 4.1.

4. Checks requested in the study brief

4.1 County of residence

DSHS and CDC both state that counts are by the recipient's county of residence. Three findings limit that:

  • People with no usable address are in "Other": 1.2% of first-dose recipients after the April 2021 fix, 3.0% by 2023.
  • County is assigned by address matching. DSHS told NBC 5 DFW in July 2021 that the first step uses ZIP codes and that ZIP codes crossing county lines had put some people in the wrong county. The records give no way to measure this; it would mostly move people between adjacent counties in metropolitan areas.
  • In 16 counties recorded adult first doses exceed the adult population estimate at some edition (county_flags.csv): Maverick, Reeves, Webb, Brooks, Dimmit, Presidio, Hidalgo, Loving, Hudspeth, Starr, Cameron, Frio, Val Verde, El Paso, Willacy and Duval. They hold 8.8% of Texans aged 16+ but account for 10-14% of adult first doses recorded statewide in mid-2021, 35% in January-February 2022 and 55% between November 2022 and May 2023. The files do not say who these recipients were; the geography points to people who were not usual residents. Those counties are kept out of the uptake models and shown separately. Neighbouring counties below 100% (La Salle 97%, Jim Hogg 93%, Zavala 92%) may be affected to a smaller degree and are kept.

4.2 Age eligibility

All Texans aged 16+ became eligible on 29 March 2021 (DSHS news release of 23 March 2021, source I), so eligibility did not change for the 16+ group at any point in the modelled period of the main analysis. The records agree: no one under 16 appears before the 25 May 2021 edition and no one under 12 before 12 November 2021.

Before 29 March 2021 eligibility was restricted in phases (source I): health-care workers and long-term-care residents from 14 December 2020, people aged 65+ and people with a chronic medical condition from late December, school and child-care staff from 3 March, people aged 50-64 from 15 March. By DSHS's own county estimates the first two phases covered at most 60% of adults statewide. This matters only for the extension to early 2021, which sets it out in its section 4.

4.3 Missing or suppressed values

DSHS: none; every county has a count in every edition. CDC: see source C. ACS: one county (source F).

4.4 Retrospective revisions

  • 15 April 2021 edition: 662,780 people left the "Other" row and county counts rose by 807,099 in one day, against about 135,000 on the days either side (verify_13a, verify_13b). The jump beyond a county's usual daily change, as a share of its previous count: median 4.3%, 5th-95th percentile -0.2% to 16.9%, above 10% in 60 counties.
  • 24 March 2021: Tarrant County's count fell by 62,699 in a day and rose by 59,951 the next day; in all, county counts fell by 81,391 in 78 counties in that edition. A one-day fault, not a correction.
  • 12 to 27 February 2021: the "Other" row fell from 268,993 to 160,979 while reporting continued, so at least 108,000 earlier records were given a county in those two weeks.
  • Corrections continued in smaller numbers after 15 April: 17 counties had a lower adult count on 23 April than on 20 April, and 12 had a lower count on 25 May than on 23 April (Cochran fell from 2,192 to 834). The daily copies show that these movements ended around 10 May: falls in county counts sum to 47,901 persons between 16 April and 11 May and to 757 in the two weeks after (early_events.csv).
  • From 25 May 2021 to 24 September 2021 no county's adult count fell between editions. Later falls are small (2 to 239 persons per edition statewide) and concentrated in counties with fewer than 5,000 adults (verify_14).

4.5 Reporting backlogs

See source A (reporting lag) and verify_06. First doses given in January 2021 numbered 1,754,542 in the 25 May 2021 edition and 1,827,899 in the final edition, a 4% later addition. Counts in this study are by report date.

4.6 Rounded or capped percentages

The sources used for modelling give counts, so no percentage is used. Where percentages are all that exist (CDC's capped fields, Harris County's two-decimal series) they were documented and set aside.

5. Outcome definitions

  • Recorded coverage (observed, not modelled): residents aged 16+ recorded with at least one dose by the edition's data-through date, divided by the Census estimate of residents aged 16+ on 1 July 2021. People of unknown age (never more than 0.07% of the total) are counted with adults.
  • Uptake in an interval (modelled): adults newly recorded with a first dose during the interval, divided by adults not yet recorded as vaccinated at its start. Reported per 30 days.
  • A county-interval enters the model only if the county never exceeds 100% recorded coverage and recorded coverage at the start of the interval is below 95%. Where a county's count fell between editions, new first doses are set to zero and the row is flagged (40 of 2,855 rows, 257 persons in total).

Both quantities describe vaccine receipt as recorded in the state registry. They say nothing about willingness or acceptance, which would need survey data.
